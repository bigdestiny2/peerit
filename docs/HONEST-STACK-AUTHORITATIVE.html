Why this document exists

The two companion explainers (Blind Substrate and BlindShard & Pure Pipe) each contain lines that overstate what's real today — most dangerously, the claim that "the reader's browser runs a real HyperDHT node." A normal browser physically cannot do this. That's not a gap to close with more code; it's a hard limit of the web platform. Repeating it in public would cost the project credibility it can't recover.

At the time of this snapshot, this document was the authoritative correction. It catalogs every overstatement found in the companion docs, presents the strongest defensible version of each claim, and shows the full architecture honestly distinguished by what ships today vs what's target vs what's fantasy.

01The four statuses (read this first)

Every claim below carries one of four badges. The badge is the contract.

StatusMeaningWhat you can say
Shipped Running in production on the live peerit.site deployment, today. "peerit does X." Present tense. Defensible.
Cutover Actively deploying right now — code shipping to the live fleet, peerit being stood up on it, but not yet converged end-to-end at the moment of writing. "peerit is cutting over to X." Present-progressive. Promote to Shipped only after live convergence is proven.
Coded, disabled Code exists, is tested (often against a fake fleet), but is turned off in production pending a deployment gate (independent operators, upstream lib maturity, etc.). "peerit has built X; it activates when Y." Never present tense.
Spec'd Designed in a reviewed document, not yet implemented. The master spec is explicit that it "does not describe the current production deployment." "the design calls for X." Never "peerit does X."
Fantasy Architecturally impossible on the target platform. No amount of code fixes it. The founder was explicitly talked out of pursuing it. Never say this. If tempted, re-read the founder's note in §03.

The original sin this prevents

Every "decentralized" product that lost credibility did it the same way: described the target state as the current state, in present tense, in public, until someone checked. The four-status system exists to make that drift impossible. If you can't badge a claim, you can't make it.

04The strongest honest claims

What we can actually defend, in public, today. Each claim is followed by the evidence and the honest ceiling.

The defensible present-tense claims Shipped

  1. "Every record is Ed25519-signed and re-verified in your browser. No relay can forge content."
    Evidence: live in production. Ceiling: a relay can still withhold or serve stale data — signatures protect integrity, not availability.
  2. "In PearBrowser, you're a real peer — direct Hyperswarm, no relay in your path, no origin that can be seized."
    Evidence: PearBrowser runs on the Pear/Bare runtime with real UDP sockets. Ceiling: only PearBrowser users get this; normal browser users do not.
  3. "Storage keys are opaque — an operator cannot enumerate the social graph by reading disk."
    Evidence: v2 opaque keys (HMAC'd) are live. Ceiling: the outer record metadata (app, namespace, type, author-signing) is still visible in today's OutboxLog — the full substrate spec blinds those too, but isn't shipped.
  4. "Posting costs compute (proof-of-work), not accounts. There's no signup, no email, no captcha."
    Evidence: live. Ceiling: PoW raises cost; it doesn't prove personhood. A funded Sybil is still possible.
  5. "Moderation is a signed overlay — mods can't edit your posts, only clients choose to honor mod actions."
    Evidence: live. Ceiling: clients can choose not to honor; there's no global enforcement.
  6. "The web bundle is verifiable against an offline signing key — mirrors and auditors can confirm a bundle is authentic without trusting the origin."
    Evidence: signed-release chain. Ceiling: a first-time visitor still downloads the verifier from the origin; full protection requires external verification or PearBrowser.

The defensible cutover claims In cutover

Each is true as the cutover converges. Use present-progressive tense, not present.

  1. "The blind substrate is going live — peerit is moving from app-aware OutboxLog onto the strict membrane, on 2 relays." Cutover
    Promote to Shipped when: live convergence proofs confirm peerit reads/writes through Blind Cells on both relays.
  2. "Adding a new app is becoming zero-config — the substrate is app-agnostic by construction, not by namespace registration." Cutover
    Promote to Shipped when: a second conforming app (e.g. p2pbuilders) is stood up on the same 2 relays without relay-side config changes.
  3. "Multi-relay reconciliation is going live — two independent relays, cross-head verification, no single ingress." Cutover
    Promote to Shipped when: the singleIngressWriter: true flag is cleared and write-path failover is demonstrated across both relays.

The defensible future-tense claims

Each requires naming the gate.

  1. "When ≥3 independent operators join the shard cohort, no single operator will hold a readable, complete, or linkable post body." Coded, disabled
    Gate: recruiting independent operators (a social/deployment problem, not code).
  2. "When the blind substrate ships, adding a new app will require zero relay configuration." Spec'd
    Gate: implementing hiverelay-blind/1 and migrating peerit off OutboxLog.
  3. "When dht-relay-ws reaches production, normal browsers will reach the DHT through a blind byte-pipe that carries opaque frames and stores nothing readable." Coded, disabled
    Gate: upstream @hyperswarm/dht-relay reaching production; not a peerit problem alone.
    Honest framing: "blind byte-pipe," not "browser is a peer." There's still a relay in the path; it's just blind.

The claims you must never make

Forbidden

  • "The browser runs a real HyperDHT node." Fantasy
  • "No relay in the path." False for browsers
  • "The operator can't read your posts." False — they can join as a reader
  • "It's anonymous." False — metadata leaks
  • "It can't be taken down." False — single origin today
  • "Blind" without naming a guarantee level. Forbidden by spec

06Zoom: cell & capability anatomy

The target storage unit — what a "cell" actually is in the spec, and how it differs from what's shipped today.

The read capability is the key abstraction in both. It's the slot address plus the decryption key, held only by the client, never on the relay. Without it, the bytes are unreadable. The capability threads through encrypted "next pointers" between cells, so a reader with the bootstrap capability can follow the chain — but the relay sees only disjoint opaque blobs.

Why the spec'd cell is stronger

Today's OutboxLog record blinds the graph keys (good) but leaves app/namespace/type visible in the outer fields. The target cell moves everything app-specific inside the ciphertext and addresses the cell by a random slot derived from a time bucket and a one-time create key. An operator looking at the filesystem sees a sea of identical-looking boxes at random addresses — no prefix enumeration, no type discrimination, no app fingerprint.

10The permanent ceilings

What no amount of code or deployment will ever change. Read these before any claim.

  1. A normal browser cannot become a real DHT node. Hard platform limit: no UDP, no listen sockets, no routing slot. The honest target is a blind relay, not no relay.
  2. Public content is never secret from a determined reader. The read key reaches everyone; an operator who joins as a reader can reconstruct. Blindness is deniability + no passive index, never confidentiality.
  3. Metadata always leaks. Sizes, timing, IPs (without Tor), access patterns, per-author record counts. No layer of this stack hides them fully.
  4. Collusion across K operators reconstructs anything. BlindShard's threshold is a coordination bar, not a cryptographic wall. Defense is jurisdictional diversity.
  5. The entry point is a chokepoint. peerit.site is a single origin today. Dispersal resists seizure of storage, not blocking of the bundle origin. The signed-roster-inline fix is still ahead.
  6. No password recovery. Your key is your identity. Lose the key and the backup phrase, and it's gone. PearBrowser gives you a BIP-39 phrase; that's the floor.
  7. PoW raises cost; it doesn't prove personhood. Identities are free to mint. Votes are advisory, not one-human-one-vote.
  8. Name squatting at genesis. First claimant of a new community name wins it. You can't hijack an established one, but you can squat a new one. Zooko's triangle, unsolved.

The contract

Every claim made about this stack — in docs, in marketing, in casual conversation — must clear three bars:

  1. Badged with its real status (shipped / coded-disabled / spec'd / fantasy).
  2. Conditional if not shipped ("when X ships, Y"), never present-tense for targets.
  3. Ceilinged — the honest limit is named alongside the claim, not buried in a footnote.

The original sin of decentralized projects is describing the target as the present. This stack exists to be different. Don't be the one who drifts.
