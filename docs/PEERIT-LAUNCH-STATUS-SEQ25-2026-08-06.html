01What happened, in one paragraph

After the vNext cutover shipped, one defect remained: a first-time visitor (no cached floor) sometimes got the empty "blocked" page instead of the seeded boards. The cause was not a bug in verification — it was liveness fragility: the cold boot walks each relay's signed descriptor chain link-by-link (23 links Sydney, 20 Dallas, growing hourly) and then reads 39 cells, and the client treated any single network hiccup as fatal to the whole walk. A dropped connection, a WiFi handoff, or the relay edge closing a response mid-body during a load window was enough to strand a new visitor. Sequence 25 makes the walk and the cell reads retry only those transient events, bounded (6 attempts, ~12s window), while every signature, chain-continuity, genesis, admission, and epoch check remains exactly as strict — a retried response is still verified byte-for-byte before it is accepted, and a verification failure is never retried.

Live now: seq-25 bundle 0256300f… served from peerit.site (auto-deploy from main, no manual step), CI green on merge commit 2ec2a3d, relays healthy and rotating on their normal hourly cadence.

02The symptom and the hunt

The visible symptom was a coin flip: some first visits rendered all 11 communities and 17 posts; others showed the empty lurker page with blocked-seed-recovery. Refreshing usually fixed it — which made it easy to miss and hard to catch. It was caught properly only by a purpose-built soak harness that cold-boots a fresh browser context against the live relays, ten times per run.

What the evidence showed

  • Never a timeout. No deadline ever fired naturally. The failures were single, sub-second transport events: Failed to fetch, an aborted describe, or — the subtle one — a truncated HTTP body, where the relay edge closes the connection mid-response and the client reads fewer bytes than the response declared.
  • The walk had zero retry. One bad describe out of ~49 was enough to fail the entire cold recovery. That is the right posture for a verification failure — but a dropped connection is not a verification failure, and the client was treating them identically.
  • A relay degradation window made it visible. On 2026-08-05 between 19:00–21:00 UTC both relay edges logged public response write timed out and IPC aborts — under load the edge gave up writing some responses and cut connections mid-body. During that window a baseline soak blocked 5–7 of 10 boots. Outside it, blocks were rarer but still present.
The truncation subtlety: the vendored blind client classifies a truncated body as RELAY_PROTOCOL_VIOLATION ("response is shorter than the selected class") — the same error class as a genuinely malformed or dishonest relay response. The fix scopes the retry to the exact truncated-body message and nothing else; every other violation in that class still fails closed forever, and a test pins the exact vendored strings so a future client rebuild can't silently reword them.

06Honest edges

  • KNOWN Relay degradation windows are a relay-lane item. The 2026-08-05 19:00–21:00 UTC window (edge write timeouts + daemon IPC aborts on both relays) is recorded in the seq-25 decision follow-ups. The client now survives it; the relays should stop causing it. Edge write-timeout and daemon-IPC tuning is queued for the relay lane.
  • KNOWN First-visit boot takes ~60–100s. That is the price of zero-trust cold start: walk two chains to genesis, verify every link, read 39 cells, verify every record. Returning visits are instant (cached floor). The structural fix — bootstrap sharding / compressed chain checkpoints — is roadmap track 2, not a patch.
  • KNOWN browser-smoke CI flake. The first-comment-write step fails ~2/3 of runs and passes on rerun (it failed once on this merge and passed on rerun). It is a test-harness timing issue, not a product defect; tracked for a proper fix.
