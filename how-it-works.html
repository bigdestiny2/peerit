peer-to-peer communities, explained

A Reddit-shaped community network with no company in the middle.

peerit has communities, posts, comments, votes, feeds, and moderation. The difference is underneath: each person signs their own activity, peers carry copies, and your device checks the result.

How it works

One action becomes a shared, verifiable conversation.

There is no central server deciding what happened. The record proves who made it, and every reader applies the same public rules.

01

Write & sign

You create a post, comment, vote, edit, or moderation action. Your private key signs it on your device.

02

Publish your outbox

The signed action is appended to your personal log. You remain the authoritative writer for your activity.

03

Replicate

PearBrowser peers copy outboxes directly. Optional relays or seeders can keep the same signed data available.

04

Verify

Each reader checks signatures, sequence, proof of work, community rules, and moderation authority.

05

Assemble your feed

Your device merges valid records into threads and ranks the feed with visible, deterministic logic.

The important shift: the network helps records arrive; signatures and deterministic rules decide whether they are valid.

Three ways to reach the same network

The trust model stays the same across every route.

PearBrowserDirect peer-to-peer

Join Hyperswarm, exchange signed outboxes with peers, keep local state, and work without an authoritative web server.

Normal browserVerified relay delivery

A configured relay carries records the browser cannot fetch from Hyperswarm directly. The client still verifies every record.

Offline or reconnectingLocal-first continuity

Read what is already on your device, queue work locally where supported, then reconcile valid records when peers return.

Serverless, honestly

No central authority does not mean no infrastructure.

Peers still need routes and replicas. The key distinction is that infrastructure can improve delivery without gaining the power to author your activity or silently rewrite community history.

A relay can withhold, not forge

A relay may be slow, unavailable, or selective. It cannot produce a valid signature for somebody else's identity.

Availability is not authority

More replicas make data easier to reach. They do not make invalid data true or grant moderation power.

The web origin still matters

In a normal browser, the site origin delivers the client code. PearBrowser is the fully peer-to-peer application path.
