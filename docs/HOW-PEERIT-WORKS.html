The whole idea in 60 seconds

Peerit replaces one company database with many signed records. The app on your device turns those records into the forum you see.

01 / WRITE

You control your identity

Peerit creates a cryptographic key on your device. When you post, comment, vote, or moderate, that key signs the record as yours.

02 / CARRY

The network moves records

Peers or relays copy signed records around. They help with delivery and availability; they do not get to decide whether a record is genuine.

03 / CHECK

Your device builds the view

Your client checks every record, keeps the valid ones, and assembles communities, threads, vote totals, rankings, and moderation locally.

What Peerit proves — and what it doesn’t

Cryptography solves specific trust problems. It does not make the real world disappear.

✓ Protected by the design

  • ✓
    AuthorshipA valid signature ties a record to the key that created it.
  • ✓
    Tamper detectionChanging signed data breaks the checks and the client rejects it.
  • ✓
    Client-side feed assemblyCommunities, threads, rankings, and totals are built from checked records.
  • ✓
    Signed moderationModerator actions are records too, and clients verify the authority chain.

! Still depends on the world

  • !
    AvailabilityA relay or peer may be offline, blocked, or refuse to serve data.
  • !
    Metadata privacyPeerit is not an anonymity system; network observers can still learn things.
  • !
    Web code deliveryA first-time web visitor still receives JavaScript from an origin.
  • !
    Independent operatorsThe current public test uses two owner-operated relays, not an independent fleet.

Integrity is not the same as availability or anonymity.

Current public test: two owner-operated relays. Browser posting is a next milestone.

Identity keyYour unforgeable signing pen+

Peerit uses an Ed25519 key pair. The private half signs your actions and stays on your device; the public half lets anyone check them.

Signed recordOne checkable action: a post, vote, reply, or moderation decision+

A record contains the action’s data plus an author key and signature. Readers do not need to trust the machine that delivered it.

OutboxA person’s append-only stream of signed records+

In the peer-to-peer model, each writer owns their own stream. Other devices copy and combine many writers’ streams instead of asking one central database.

RelayA courier, not the judge of truth+

A relay helps browser users move or retrieve data. It may withhold or delay data, but it cannot create a record that passes someone else’s signature check.

Blind cellAn opaque, fixed-format storage unit+

The public browser path fetches generic cells and checks the expected hashes locally. The relay serves storage units without deciding what a Peerit post means.

This explainer describes Peerit’s current public-test boundary: direct peer-to-peer reading and writing in PearBrowser, and verified read-only cold recovery in stock browsers.
